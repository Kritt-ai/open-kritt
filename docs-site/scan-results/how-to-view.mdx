Once a scan is running, open it to watch progress and browse findings as they land. The
**Overview** dashboard and the **Scans** list are your entry points.

## The scan detail page

Open any scan to see:

- **Header** - the target repo (with a remote/local badge), the workflow, model, harness,
  thinking effort, and commit.
- **Stats** - number of findings, how many are confirmed exploitable, the primary
  post-script, and how many agent skills were used.
- **Status** - a live badge (`queued`, `pending`, `prewarming_cache`, `running`,
  `rate_limited`, `post_processing`, `completed`, `paused`, `stopped`, or `failed`) plus
  available controls to pause, resume, or export. Queued scans wait for active scans;
  rate-limited scans show when their automatic retry is due.
- **Findings** - the list of vulnerabilities, ordered by your ranker and annotated with
  [chips](/post-scripts/chips) from post-scripts. Findings with post-processing added after
  the scan show a `+post ×N` indicator that remains cumulative across multiple runs. On a
  gated workflow every finding also shows a `lifecycle` chip
  ([investigation kind and readiness](/scans/investigation-kind-and-readiness)).
- **Scan graph** - the workflow drawn as one box per step, in columns by depth. Each box
  shows completed over expected lineages, a bar of completed, running, failed, and
  interrupted attempts, and how many records (or findings, for the last depth) the step
  emitted. Edges are labelled with the lineages that flowed between two steps; a dashed
  edge means some expected lineages have not been attempted yet. Below the steps, the
  post-processing funnel shows raw, canonical, and duplicate findings, then each
  post-script with its attempt counts and a breakdown of result values such as
  `poc_status` or `verdict`; the gated D3, D4, and D5 stages break down by
  `_engine_lifecycle.lifecycle_status` instead. The panel refreshes every two seconds
  while the scan is active and can be collapsed.

## The findings list

Each row summarizes a finding - its title, location, and a few chips. Findings are sorted by
the ranking rules you set on the scan, so the highest-priority results sit at the top.

Click any finding to open the [vulnerability view](/scan-results/vulnerability-view).

## The Evidence card

On a workflow with a readiness gate, the finding page shows an **Evidence** card above the
post-script cards whenever an engine block (`_engine_lifecycle`, `_engine_evidence`, or
`_engine_readiness`) exists for the finding:

- **Dimensions table** - Bug, Impact, Terminal outcome, Deployment (open material
  assumptions), Scope, Novelty, and Readiness (label, ready, first blocking reason), each
  as its own state. A reproduced bug with an unproven impact shows exactly that.
- **Required versus observed** - the D3 `terminal_outcome` beside the D4
  `observed_terminal_outcome`.
- **Impact chain** - one row per hop: id, claim, status, covered evidence dimensions,
  assessment, and each evidence path marked **captured** or **unresolved** against the
  artifact manifest.
- **Missing links, assumptions, and mapping** - the D4 `missing_impact_links`, the
  structured assumptions with their `material` and `status` flags, and the D5
  required-versus-observed `impact_mapping`.
- **Blocking reasons** - the full list from the engine decision, each with the check name
  that failed.

A **legacy** badge marks a finding whose blocks were synthesized at read time because the
scan predates the gate; such findings are always impact-unverified and never ready. The
generic post-script card hides `_engine_*` keys, `model_readiness_claim`, and
`_chip_lifecycle`; the Evidence card is where they are shown. See
[investigation kind and readiness](/scans/investigation-kind-and-readiness) for the
lifecycle statuses and the numbered readiness rules.

## Exports

The result ZIP reflects the engine decision for gated scans:

- `finding.md` gains a **Readiness** chapter: the dimensions table, required and observed
  outcome, the evidence chain with captured status, negative control, repeatability,
  assumptions, missing links, limitations (`remaining_limits`), and the readiness
  decision with its blocking reasons and policy version.
- `report.txt` keeps the model's prose unchanged but starts with a fenced header:
  `NOT READY — <label> — <lifecycle> — reasons: …` when the engine decision is not ready,
  or `READY under <policy_version>` when it is.
- `manifest.json` records `readiness: { ready, label, lifecycleStatus, policyVersion,
  legacy }` per finding, and the README table replaces the PoC yes/no column with a
  lifecycle column plus a count of findings per lifecycle status.
- `post-processing.json` is unchanged; it already carries the raw results including the
  engine blocks.

On a terminal-only host, `./kritt-headless scan show <id>` displays scan state, progress,
active execution stages, and recent failure reasons without printing finding contents.
Use `./kritt-headless scan export <id>` to save the result ZIP to the repository root, or
provide a destination directory or `.zip` path. See the [headless CLI guide](/getting-started/headless-cli).

<Note>
  Findings appear as the scan progresses - you don't have to wait for `completed` to start
  triaging what's already come in.
</Note>
